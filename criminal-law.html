Our Criminal Defence Strategy

Every case is different, but our approach is always methodical, aggressive, and client-focused.

  1. Immediate Action We respond to your call immediately. In emergencies, we can have a lawyer at the police station or court within hours to protect your rights from the very first moment.
  2. Thorough Investigation We conduct our own independent investigation, examining every piece of evidence, interviewing witnesses, and identifying weaknesses in the prosecution's case.
  3. Challenge the Prosecution We scrutinise the State's evidence for procedural irregularities, constitutional violations, and evidentiary gaps. If evidence was obtained unlawfully, we fight to have it excluded.
  4. Build Your Defence We construct a compelling defence strategy tailored to your specific circumstances, supported by evidence, expert testimony, and persuasive legal argument.
  5. Negotiate When Appropriate Where it serves your best interest, we negotiate with prosecutors for reduced charges, alternative sentencing, or diversion programs that can keep your record clean.
  6. Fight in Court If your case goes to trial, we are prepared, formidable courtroom advocates who will fight relentlessly to achieve the best possible outcome for you.

What to Do If You're Arrested

Stay Calm. Protect Your Rights. Call Us.

  1. 1
    Remain Silent: You have the constitutional right not to answer any questions. Politely state that you wish to remain silent until your lawyer is present.
  2. 2
    Don't Resist Arrest: Even if you believe the arrest is unlawful, do not physically resist. Resistance can lead to additional charges and injury.
  3. 3
    Contact Us Immediately: Call or have someone call Linda Chirwa Attorneys as soon as possible.
    074 990 5872
  4. 4
    Don't Consent to Searches: You are not required to consent to a search of your person, vehicle, or home without a warrant. Politely decline and let your attorney handle it.
  5. 5
    Remember Details: Take mental note of officer names, badge numbers, the time and location of the arrest, and any witnesses present.
  6. 6
    Don't Discuss Your Case: Do not discuss the details of your case with anyone except your attorney. This includes fellow detainees, family members over monitored calls, or police officers.
  7. 7
    Don't Post on Social Media: Anything you post online can and will be used against you. Refrain from posting about your arrest, charges, or case on any social media platform.

Traffic Offence Defence Tips

During a Traffic Stop

  • Pull over safely and remain calm
  • Keep your hands visible on the steering wheel
  • Provide your licence and registration when requested
  • Be polite but do not volunteer information
  • You have the right to decline field sobriety tests (though this may have consequences)
  • Note the officer's name, badge number, and details of the stop
  • Do not argue or become confrontational

Fighting Traffic Tickets

Not all traffic tickets need to result in fines, demerit points, or a criminal record. Many traffic offences can be successfully challenged on procedural grounds, calibration issues with speed measuring equipment, incorrect signage, or improper documentation. Contact us before simply paying the fine, paying is an admission of guilt. We assess every case to determine the best strategy for reducing or eliminating the charges against you.

Criminal Defence FAQs

Should I talk to the police without a lawyer present?

No. You have a constitutional right to remain silent and to have a lawyer present during questioning. Anything you say to the police can be used against you in court. Always exercise your right to silence and contact an attorney before making any statements. Even seemingly innocent statements can be misinterpreted or used out of context.

What happens if I can't afford bail?

If bail is set at an amount you cannot afford, your attorney can argue for a reduction. The court must consider your financial circumstances when setting bail. We can also explore alternatives such as release on warning, or present arguments for why a lower bail amount would still ensure your attendance at trial. In some cases, bail can be paid in instalments.

Will I go to jail if convicted?

Not necessarily. Many criminal convictions do not result in imprisonment. Depending on the nature and severity of the offence, alternatives such as fines, suspended sentences, community service, correctional supervision, or house arrest may be available. Our attorneys work hard during sentencing to present compelling mitigating factors and argue for the least restrictive sentence possible.

Can criminal charges be dropped?

Yes, charges can be dropped or withdrawn for various reasons, including insufficient evidence, procedural errors by the police or prosecution, successful diversion applications, or when the complainant withdraws. Our defence strategy always includes identifying opportunities for charges to be reduced or withdrawn entirely.

What is the difference between Magistrate's Court and High Court?

Magistrate's Courts handle less serious offences and have limited sentencing powers (generally up to a certain number of years imprisonment depending on the court level). The High Court handles serious offences such as murder, rape, and treason, and has unlimited sentencing powers. The court in which your case is heard depends on the severity of the charges. We represent clients in both courts.

How long does a criminal case take?

The duration varies significantly depending on the complexity of the case, the court's schedule, the number of witnesses, and various procedural factors. Simple matters may be resolved in weeks, while complex cases can take months or even years. We keep you informed at every stage and work to resolve your matter as efficiently as possible without compromising the quality of your defence.
