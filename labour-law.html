What We Handle

Unfair Dismissal

A dismissal is fair only if there is a fair reason and a fair procedure. The Labour Relations Act 66 of 1995 protects employees from dismissal for misconduct, incapacity, poor performance or operational reasons where the employer has not met that standard.

What we handle

  • Misconduct dismissals and the fairness of the disciplinary hearing
  • Dismissal for poor performance or incapacity, including ill health
  • Automatically unfair dismissals, such as dismissal for pregnancy, union activity or making a protected disclosure
  • Constructive dismissal, where an employee resigns because the employer made continued employment intolerable
  • Dismissal of employees on fixed-term contracts and repeated renewals
  • Defending employers against dismissal claims

A dismissal dispute must be referred to the CCMA within 30 days of the dismissal. Late referrals need condonation, which is not guaranteed. Do not wait.

Unfair Labour Practices

An unfair labour practice is unfair conduct by an employer about promotion, demotion, training, benefits, suspension, discipline short of dismissal, or the provision of reasons for a decision.

What we handle

  • Unfair suspension, demotion and disciplinary action
  • Unfair refusal to promote or to reinstate or re-employ
  • Benefits and pay disputes that amount to an unfair labour practice
  • Occupational detriment after making a protected disclosure
  • Defending employers against these claims

An unfair labour practice must usually be referred within 90 days of the act or omission.

CCMA Conciliation & Arbitration

The Commission for Conciliation, Mediation and Arbitration hears most employment disputes first. There is no fee to refer a dispute.

How the process works

  • The dispute is referred, and a conciliation is set down, usually within about 30 days
  • A commissioner tries to settle the matter at conciliation
  • If it does not settle, the matter goes to arbitration, where evidence is led and the commissioner issues a binding award
  • In some cases the matter goes to the Labour Court instead

How we help

  • Referring the dispute correctly and in time, including condonation applications
  • Preparing your case, witnesses and documents
  • Representing you at arbitration where the rules allow legal representation
  • Reviewing or enforcing awards

Legal representation is not allowed at conciliation, and at arbitration of misconduct and incapacity dismissals it needs the commissioner's permission. We prepare you thoroughly for those hearings.

Retrenchment & Operational Requirements

Retrenchment must follow a consultation process before anyone is dismissed. Section 189 of the Labour Relations Act sets out what employers must do, and larger exercises have extra requirements.

For employers

  • Planning a lawful and defensible retrenchment
  • Section 189 notices, consultation and selection criteria
  • Calculating and recording severance pay
  • Defending retrenchment disputes

For employees

  • Checking whether the process and selection were fair
  • Severance pay, which is at least one week's remuneration for each completed year of service
  • Challenging a retrenchment that is really a disguised dismissal
Discrimination, Harassment & Occupational Detriment

The Employment Equity Act 55 of 1998 prohibits unfair discrimination in employment, and workplace harassment can ground a claim against the employer.

What we handle

  • Claims of discrimination on listed grounds such as race, gender, disability, age and pregnancy
  • Sexual harassment complaints and the employer's duty to act
  • Equal pay disputes
  • Protected disclosure and whistleblower protection claims
Wages, Leave & Contract Claims

The Basic Conditions of Employment Act 75 of 1997 sets minimum standards on working time, leave, notice and pay.

What we handle

  • Unpaid wages, overtime, leave pay and notice pay
  • Breach of an employment contract
  • Disputes over bonuses, commission and deductions
  • Recovery through the CCMA, the Labour Court or the civil courts

How We Work

  1. Quick assessment We check the dates first, because deadlines decide what is still possible, then the merits and the likely outcome.
  2. Refer the dispute We refer the matter to the CCMA or the bargaining council, with condonation if the referral is late.
  3. Conciliation We negotiate for a settlement. Many disputes end here.
  4. Arbitration or Labour Court If there is no settlement, we prepare your evidence and argue the matter.
  5. Award and enforcement We enforce the award, or review it where the commissioner got it wrong.

What to Bring to Your First Consultation

Having these ready lets us give you a faster, more useful assessment. We will confirm exactly what is needed once we understand your situation.

  • Your contract of employment and any policies you were given
  • The dismissal letter, notice of hearing and the outcome
  • The date you were dismissed or the act you are complaining about
  • Payslips for the last three months
  • Emails, messages or minutes that support your version
  • Names of witnesses
  • Any warnings or performance reviews
  • Details of your length of service and your last salary
  • Any CCMA or bargaining council documents already received

Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to challenge a dismissal?

Thirty days from the date of dismissal to refer an unfair dismissal dispute to the CCMA or the relevant bargaining council. Late referrals need condonation. Speak to us as soon as you are dismissed.

What can I get if the dismissal was unfair?

The commissioner can order reinstatement or re-employment, or compensation. Compensation for an unfair dismissal is capped at 12 months' remuneration, and at 24 months for an automatically unfair dismissal.

Do I need a lawyer at the CCMA?

You do not have to have one, and the rules limit legal representation at conciliation and at some arbitrations. Preparation by an attorney still makes a real difference to how your case is presented.

Can I be dismissed during probation?

Probation does not remove your protection. A dismissal must still be for a fair reason and follow a fair process, although the standard applied to a probationer can differ.

What is constructive dismissal?

It is where an employee resigns because the employer has made continued employment intolerable, and the employee can show that the employer's conduct, not their own choice, ended the employment.

Does my employer have to hold a hearing before dismissing me?

A dismissal for misconduct must follow a fair procedure, which usually means notice of the allegations and an opportunity to respond. The form can vary with the size of the business and the seriousness of the matter.
