Immigration & Permits

Visas, permanent residence, citizenship and appeals, prepared carefully so your application is complete the first time.

Home / Practice Areas / Immigration & Permits

Immigration applications are decided on paper, and a missing document or an incorrect form can mean a refusal and months lost. We assess whether you qualify before you apply, prepare a complete application, and guide you through the process and any appeal. We act for individuals, families and employers across South Africa. Processing times are set by the Department of Home Affairs and we cannot control them, but we can make sure your file is in order.

What We Handle

Temporary Residence Visas

The Immigration Act 13 of 2002 provides for several categories of temporary residence. Which one fits depends on why you are coming and for how long.

Common categories

  • Visitor's visas for tourism, short business trips and similar purposes
  • Study visas for learners and students
  • Work visas, including critical skills, general work and intra-company transfer visas
  • Business visas for people starting or investing in a business
  • Retired person's visas
  • Medical treatment visas and exchange visas

We tell you which category fits, what evidence it needs and whether your plan is realistic before you spend money on an application.

Work Visas & Employer Support

Employers who hire foreign nationals have their own obligations, and the visa must match the job.

What we handle

  • Work visa applications for individuals and for employers
  • Documents such as the employer letter, qualifications evaluation and police clearance
  • Intra-company transfers
  • Renewals, amendments and changes of employer or conditions
  • Advising employers on compliance when employing foreign nationals
Spouse, Life Partner & Family Visas

South Africans and permanent residents can sponsor a spouse, a life partner or other close relatives, subject to proof that the relationship is genuine.

What we handle

  • Spousal and life partner visas
  • Relatives' visas
  • Proof of a life partnership, including a notarial partnership agreement from our Notarial Services
  • Visas for dependent children
Permanent Residence

Permanent residence is granted in specific categories, such as after several years on a qualifying work visa, through a relationship with a South African or permanent resident, or through a business or retirement route.

What we handle

  • Assessing which category you qualify for
  • Preparing and submitting the application and supporting evidence
  • Responding to queries and requests from the Department
  • Renewing and replacing permanent residence permits

These applications take a long time. A complete, well-organised file is the best protection against delay.

Citizenship

Citizenship may be acquired by birth, by descent or by naturalisation under the South African Citizenship Act 88 of 1995.

What we handle

  • Naturalisation applications by permanent residents who meet the residence requirements
  • Citizenship by descent and proof of a parent's citizenship
  • Registration of births abroad
  • Loss of, retention of and resumption of citizenship
Appeals, Reviews & Refusals

A refusal is not always the end. There are internal appeal routes, and a decision that was unlawful or unreasonable can be taken on review.

What we handle

  • Appeals to the Director-General against refusals, which have short deadlines
  • Further appeals to the Minister
  • Review applications in the High Court
  • Late and incomplete application issues

The deadline to appeal a refusal is short, often 10 working days from notice. If you have been refused, contact us immediately.

Overstaying & Declarations of Undesirability

Overstaying a visa, or breaching its conditions, can lead to a ban on returning to South Africa, sometimes for years.

What we handle

  • Advice on the consequences of an overstay
  • Representations and appeals against a declaration of undesirability
  • Applications for waivers and for the lifting of bans where the law allows
  • Asylum and refugee matters under the Refugees Act 130 of 1998

How We Work

  1. Eligibility assessment We review your situation and tell you which visa or permit fits, and whether you qualify.
  2. Document checklist We give you a precise list of documents and help you obtain them, including certified and apostilled copies.
  3. Preparing the application We complete the forms, assemble the file and check it against the Department's requirements.
  4. Submission and follow-up We lodge the application, usually through a VFS Global centre, track it and respond to queries.
  5. Outcome, renewal or appeal We collect the outcome and diarise renewals. If the application is refused, we assess an appeal at once.

What to Bring to Your First Consultation

Having these ready lets us give you a faster, more useful assessment. We will confirm exactly what is needed once we understand your situation.

  • Valid passport with enough blank pages and validity
  • Your current visa or permit, if you have one
  • Proof of the purpose of your stay, such as a job offer or admission letter
  • Police clearance certificates from the countries you have lived in
  • Medical and radiological reports where required
  • Proof of relationship, such as marriage certificate or partnership agreement
  • Proof of funds or financial means
  • Qualifications and, where needed, an evaluation from SAQA
  • Passport photographs and proof of address

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a visa application take?

It varies widely between categories and over time, and the Department of Home Affairs controls it. We tell you what the current experience is, but we cannot guarantee a processing time.

Can I work while my application is pending?

Not automatically. Whether you may work depends on your current visa and its conditions. Do not assume you can. Ask us before you start.

What if my application is refused?

There is a right of appeal, and the deadline is short, often 10 working days from notice of the refusal. Contact us immediately so the appeal is filed in time.

What happens if I overstay?

You can be declared undesirable and banned from returning, sometimes for several years. If you have overstayed, get advice before you leave the country or apply for anything.

Do I need documents apostilled or translated?

Often yes. Foreign documents usually need an apostille or legalisation, and documents not in English may need a sworn translation. Our notaries can arrange this through our apostille service.
