What We Handle

Choosing a Structure

South African businesses usually operate as a private company, a non-profit company, a partnership, a trust or as a sole proprietor. Each has different consequences for liability, tax and administration.

Options we advise on

  • Private company (Pty) Ltd, the most common structure for trading businesses
  • Public companies, personal liability companies and non-profit companies
  • Partnerships and sole proprietorships, and when to move from one to a company
  • Trusts for holding assets and family businesses, in coordination with our estate planning team
  • Existing close corporations, which can still operate but cannot be newly registered

We explain the trade-offs in plain terms and recommend a structure for your goals, not a default.

Registering with CIPC

Companies are registered with the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission under the Companies Act 71 of 2008.

What we handle

  • Name reservation and checking for conflicts
  • Drafting a tailored Memorandum of Incorporation
  • Registering the company, directors and shareholders
  • Registering for tax, VAT, PAYE, UIF and the Compensation Fund where applicable
  • Share certificates, registers and the initial resolutions
  • Beneficial ownership information filed with CIPC

Registration itself is often quick, but the work that matters is in the Memorandum of Incorporation and the agreements around it.

Shareholders' Agreements

The Memorandum of Incorporation is a public document. A shareholders' agreement is private, and it is where owners record what they have really agreed.

What we cover

  • Who contributes what, and who is entitled to what
  • Decision-making, deadlock and reserved matters
  • Share transfers, pre-emption rights and drag-along and tag-along rights
  • Leaving the business: death, disability, divorce and exit
  • Restraints of trade and protection of confidential information
  • Valuation and buy-out mechanisms

Most shareholder disputes are expensive because nothing was agreed at the start. A good agreement is cheapest when you do not need it.

Directors & Governance

Directors owe duties to the company and can be personally liable for breaches, including reckless trading.

What we advise on

  • Directors' duties, conflicts of interest and personal liability
  • Board and shareholder meetings, resolutions and minutes
  • Appointing and removing directors
  • Company secretary requirements
  • Financial assistance, loans to directors and related-party transactions
  • Governance policies suited to the size of the business
Annual Compliance

Companies have continuing duties, and failure to keep up can lead to deregistration and penalties.

What we handle

  • Annual returns to CIPC, due after each anniversary of incorporation
  • Updating directors, addresses and beneficial ownership information
  • Annual financial statements and audit or independent review requirements
  • Reinstating companies that have been deregistered
  • Compliance checks before a sale, financing or investment
Changes, Restructuring & Winding Up

Companies change over time. Shareholders come and go, businesses are restructured, and some are closed.

What we handle

  • Share transfers and new share issues
  • Changing a company name or amending the Memorandum of Incorporation
  • Group restructures and holding company structures
  • Voluntary deregistration and winding up
  • Business rescue and liquidation, handled with our insolvency team

How We Work

  1. Understand the plan We ask who the owners are, who funds the business, what could go wrong and where you want to be in five years.
  2. Choose the structure We recommend a structure and explain the tax and liability consequences, so you can decide with the full picture.
  3. Register and document We register the company and draft the Memorandum of Incorporation, resolutions and the shareholders' agreement.

What to Bring to Your First Consultation

Having these ready lets us give you a faster, more useful assessment. We will confirm exactly what is needed once we understand your situation.

  • Identity documents and proof of address for every director and shareholder
  • Your proposed company names, in order of preference
  • The shareholding you want for each person
  • The nature of the business and its intended activities
  • Existing tax numbers, if you already trade
  • Details of any loans or assets going into the company
  • Any existing agreements between the owners
  • Whether you expect to employ staff or register for VAT

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I register a company or trade as a sole proprietor?

A company is a separate legal person, which can limit your personal exposure for business debts, and it is easier to bring in partners. It also carries compliance duties and costs. The right choice depends on the risks of your business, your tax position and your plans.

How long does it take to register a company?

The registration itself is often completed within days, though timing depends on CIPC. Allow more time if you need a tailored Memorandum of Incorporation and a shareholders' agreement.

Do I need a shareholders' agreement?

If there is more than one shareholder, yes. Without one, the default rules apply, and they rarely fit what the owners expected, especially when a dispute arises or one shareholder wants out.

Can I be personally liable as a director?

Yes, in certain circumstances, for example for reckless or fraudulent trading, for breach of duty, or where you have signed a personal suretyship. We explain where your exposure lies.

Can I still register a close corporation?

No. New close corporations can no longer be registered, although existing ones continue and can be converted to companies.
