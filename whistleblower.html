Whistleblower & Speak-Up Channel

A confidential way to report unlawful, unethical, or improper conduct, protected under South African law.

Home / Whistleblower

Speak up. Safely.

If you have witnessed or become aware of unlawful conduct, unethical behaviour, fraud, corruption, or any other serious wrongdoing: in your workplace, in a business you deal with, or in an organisation you're connected to; you have the right to report it. This page provides a confidential channel to do so.

Reports may be made anonymously. You do not have to give your name. Where you do identify yourself, your identity will be protected to the fullest extent permitted by law.

What this channel is for

This channel exists to receive reports of conduct that is illegal, unethical, or seriously improper. It is not a general complaints channel, and it is not a substitute for legal advice on your personal matter.

Reportable conduct includes:

  • Criminal offences (fraud, theft, bribery, corruption)
  • Contraventions of law by an employer or organisation
  • Endangering the health or safety of any individual
  • Environmental damage or disregard for environmental law
  • Unfair discrimination
  • Concealment of any of the above
  • Serious misconduct or abuse of authority
  • Breaches of professional or fiduciary duty

How we handle your report

Every report submitted through this channel is reviewed by a designated recipient within the firm. We will:

  • Acknowledge receipt where contact details are provided;
  • Assess the report and, where appropriate, refer it to the relevant authority (the SAPS, the Public Protector, the Companies and Intellectual Property Commission, or another regulator);
  • Take reasonable steps to protect your identity;
  • Keep you informed of the outcome where possible.

Your legal protection

Under the Protected Disclosures Act 26 of 2000, employees who make protected disclosures in good faith are protected from occupational detriment, including dismissal, suspension, demotion, or harassment.

The Protected Disclosures Amendment Act of 2017 extended these protections and consolidated the process for making and handling disclosures.

If you suffer retaliation for making a report, you may have a separate legal claim. Contact us.

What makes a disclosure "protected"?

For a disclosure to qualify for protection under the Protected Disclosures Act, certain conditions must generally be met:

  • The disclosure must relate to one of the categories of wrongdoing listed above.
  • It must be made to an appropriate person or body, for example, a legal advisor, an employer, a member of the Cabinet or Executive Council, the Public Protector, the Auditor-General, or another prescribed body.
  • The person making the disclosure must reasonably believe that the information is substantially true.
  • The disclosure must not be made for personal gain.

Making a report in good faith: even if the concern turns out not to be substantiated, generally preserves your protection. Making a report knowingly falsely or with malice may expose you to legal consequences.

If you are unsure whether your situation qualifies, you can submit the report and let us assess it, or contact us first for a confidential discussion.
