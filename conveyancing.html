Our Conveyancing Services

Residential Property Transfers

Whether you're buying your first home, upgrading, or selling an investment property — we manage the entire transfer process on your behalf. Our team coordinates with estate agents, bond originators, the municipality, SARS, and the Deeds Office to keep the matter moving.

What We Handle

  • Drafting and vetting the offer to purchase
  • Transfer duty and VAT assessments
  • Rates and levies clearance certificates
  • Compliance certificates (electrical, plumbing, gas, beetle, electric fence)
  • Preparation of transfer documents and signing
  • Lodgement and registration at the Deeds Office

Why It Matters

A property transfer is a legal process, not a formality. Delays in lodging documents, missing signatures, or incorrect compliance certificates can push a transfer past the deadline in the sale agreement — triggering penalties or even cancellation. We stay on top of every deadline.

Bond Registrations & Cancellations

Most property purchases are financed. We register your new bond simultaneously with the transfer and, where applicable, cancel any existing bond on the property being sold.

Bond Registration

  • Acting for both the bank and the buyer
  • Preparing bond documents and signing appointments
  • Attending to guarantees and bank instructions
  • Simultaneous registration of transfer and bond

Bond Cancellations

  • Requesting cancellation figures from the existing bank
  • Attending to cancellation documents
  • Coordinating with the bond cancellation attorney
  • Ensuring the seller's bond is cancelled on the same day as transfer

Refinancing & Further Bonds

If you're refinancing an existing property, we handle the registration of a further bond so the process is completed cleanly with no gaps in your security.

Sectional Title & Complex Transfers

Sectional title transfers add layers of complexity — body corporate rules, management fees, exclusive use areas, and trustee approvals. We handle them properly.

What's Involved

  • Obtaining body corporate clearance figures
  • Sectional title participation quota and rule compliance
  • Exclusive use area transfers
  • Managing agent coordination
  • Levies and special levy clearances

Common Pitfalls We Prevent

  • Undisclosed special levies that hit after transfer
  • Non-compliance with conduct rules that delays registration
  • Unapproved alterations to sections or common property
  • Disputes over parking bays or storage areas

The Conveyancing Process

Here's what happens from the moment you accept an offer (or your offer is accepted) to the moment your name appears on the title deed.

  1. Offer to Purchase Signed The buyer and seller agree on price, terms, and conditions. We review the offer, confirm suspensive conditions (like bond approval), and open the transfer file.
  2. Bond Application & Approval If the buyer is financing the purchase, a bond application is submitted to the bank. Once approved, the bank issues a bond grant letter and appoints us as bond attorneys.
  3. FICA & Compliance Certificates Both parties submit FICA documentation. The seller arranges compliance certificates (electrical, plumbing, gas, beetle, electric fence) as required by the sale agreement.
  4. Transfer Documents Prepared We draft the transfer documents, calculate transfer duty, request rates and levy clearance figures from the municipality and body corporate, and arrange signing appointments.
  5. Guarantees & Payment The buyer's bank issues guarantees for the purchase price. The buyer pays the deposit and costs into our trust account. Funds are held securely until registration.
  6. Lodgement at the Deeds Office Once all documents are signed and clearances received, we lodge the transfer and bond at the Deeds Office for examination and registration.
  7. Registration & Payout On the registration date, the property is transferred into the buyer's name, the bond is registered, the seller's bond is cancelled, and all parties are paid out.

Buyer's Checklist

Documents and decisions to have ready before you sign an offer to purchase.

  • Valid ID or passport (and your spouse's, if married)
  • Marriage certificate or antenuptial contract, if applicable
  • Proof of residence (not older than 3 months)
  • Proof of income — payslips, bank statements, or financials
  • Bond pre-approval from your bank or bond originator
  • Deposit funds available
  • Confirmed budget for transfer costs, bond costs, and duty
  • Understanding of the property's rates, levies, and compliance status
  • Confirmed occupation date and occupational rent arrangement
  • Awareness of any defects disclosed in the offer
  • Insurance arranged for the property from date of registration
  • Your conveyancer's contact details saved

Conveyancing FAQs

How long does a property transfer take?

Typically between 6 and 12 weeks from the date the sale agreement is signed, provided all parties cooperate and the bank issues bond approval promptly. Delays usually arise from outstanding compliance certificates, late FICA documentation, or municipal delays in issuing rates clearances. We track every deadline and follow up proactively to keep the process moving.

Who pays the conveyancing fees — buyer or seller?

By convention in South Africa, the buyer pays the transfer costs (conveyancing fees, transfer duty, and Deeds Office fees). The seller pays the estate agent's commission and is responsible for obtaining compliance certificates. However, the sale agreement can specify different arrangements. We always confirm who pays what before the transfer begins.

Can I choose my own conveyancer?

Yes. The buyer has the right to appoint the transferring attorney in most transactions. Some banks may insist on their own attorney for the bond registration, but this is separate from the transfer. If you're selling, you also have the right to appoint the conveyancer who will handle the transfer — unless you've agreed otherwise in the offer.

What is transfer duty and how is it calculated?

Transfer duty is a tax payable to SARS on the acquisition of immovable property. It's calculated on a sliding scale based on the property's value. Properties below the threshold (currently R1.1 million) pay no transfer duty. Above that, rates range from 3% to 13%. The duty is paid by the buyer before registration can take place. We calculate the exact amount for your transaction and handle the payment.

What happens if the buyer's bond is declined?

If the offer to purchase is subject to bond approval and the bond is declined within the agreed timeframe, the sale typically falls through without penalty — both parties walk away. If the buyer fails to take reasonable steps to obtain a bond, or the bond is declined for reasons within the buyer's control, the seller may have a claim for damages. This is why getting pre-approval before signing an offer is strongly recommended.

Do I need an antenuptial contract?

It depends on how you want to own property and manage your finances within the marriage. By default, South African marriages are in community of property — meaning all assets and debts are shared. An antenuptial contract (with or without accrual) allows you to keep your estates separate. The contract must be signed before the marriage and registered at the Deeds Office within three months. We prepare, attest, and register these contracts regularly.

What is a rates clearance certificate and why does it matter?

A rates clearance certificate confirms that all municipal rates and service charges (water, electricity, refuse) on the property have been paid up to date. It's required by the Deeds Office before a transfer can be registered. Municipalities typically require payment of two to six months of estimated future charges to issue the certificate. Without it, the transfer cannot proceed — and obtaining it can take weeks, so we request it as early as possible.

Can you handle a transfer in another province?

Yes. We are registered to practice in all South African Deeds Offices and have experience transferring property in Gauteng, KwaZulu-Natal, North West, and the Western Cape. For properties in other provinces, we work with trusted correspondents to ensure the matter is handled correctly.
