Civil & Commercial Litigation

When negotiation fails and a matter needs to be resolved properly: in court, through arbitration, or by a well-timed letter of demand.

At Linda Chirwa Attorneys, we handle civil disputes the way they should be handled: with early, honest advice on whether litigation is worth it, a plan for the strongest legal route, and relentless follow-through once we're instructed. Whether you're pursuing a debt, defending a claim, or trying to exit a contract that's gone wrong, we represent you at every level: Magistrate's Court, High Court, arbitration, and appeal.

What We Handle

Contract & Commercial Disputes

Most civil litigation starts with a contract that one party believes has been breached. We act for both plaintiffs and defendants, on disputes ranging from a few thousand rand to multi-million-rand commercial claims.

What We Handle

  • Breach of contract claims (sale, service, lease, supply)
  • Enforcement of restraint of trade agreements
  • Shareholder and partnership disputes
  • Director and fiduciary duty claims
  • Non-payment and repudiation disputes
  • Specific performance applications
  • Cancellation and damages claims
  • Confidentiality and non-disclosure breaches

Our Approach

We start by reading the contract, properly. Most disputes turn on a specific clause, a defined term, or a procedural requirement that was overlooked. Where the contract supports a strong claim, we pursue it aggressively. Where it doesn't, we tell you early and explore alternatives before you spend money on litigation you may not win.

Debt Collection & Recovery

Unpaid invoices, defaulting debtors, and slow-paying clients are one of the biggest cash-flow problems for South African businesses. We recover debts efficiently and, where necessary, enforce judgments.

What We Handle

  • Letters of demand and final notices
  • Summons in the Magistrate's Court and High Court
  • Default judgments and summary judgments
  • Warrants of execution against movable and immovable property
  • Garnishee orders (emoluments attachment orders)
  • Section 65 financial enquiries
  • Liquidations and sequestrations where applicable
  • Recovery of rental arrears

Credit Provider Services

We act for credit providers on bulk debt recovery portfolios, ensuring compliance with the National Credit Act while maximising recovery rates. We can handle a single defaulting debtor or a portfolio of hundreds.

Delictual Claims & Damages

A delict is a civil wrong: negligent or intentional conduct that causes harm. Delictual claims are often the most valuable claims an individual can bring, and the most complex to prove. We handle them with the rigour they require.

Personal Injury Claims

  • Motor vehicle accident claims (RAF and common-law)
  • Workplace injuries and occupational disease claims
  • Medical negligence (misdiagnosis, surgical error, birth injury)
  • Slip-and-fall and premises liability claims
  • Assault and intentional harm claims
  • Loss of support claims (dependants of a deceased breadwinner)

Property & Economic Loss Claims

  • Damage to property (vehicles, buildings, equipment)
  • Pure economic loss claims
  • Professional negligence (accountants, engineers, brokers)
  • Insurance repudiation disputes
  • Defamation and invasion of privacy
  • Fraud and misrepresentation claims

Quantum & Expert Evidence

Delictual claims require precise quantification. We work with medico-legal experts, actuaries, industrial psychologists, and forensic accountants to build a defensible case for the full extent of your damages: past, present, and future.

Urgent Applications & Motion Court

Some civil matters cannot wait for trial. Urgent applications allow you to obtain a court order in days rather than months, but they require precise legal drafting and a genuinely urgent factual foundation.

What We Handle

  • Interdicts (restraining orders in civil matters)
  • Anti-dissipation orders (freezing assets before judgment)
  • Rule 43 applications (interim maintenance and custody)
  • Spoliation applications (unlawful dispossession)
  • Mandament van spolie (unlawful deprivation of use)
  • Habeas corpus applications
  • Compelling delivery of specific performance
  • Setting aside of default judgments

Realistic Urgency

South African courts are strict about what constitutes genuine urgency. We advise clients honestly on whether their matter qualifies, and where it does, we bring the application properly, first time.

Appeals & Reviews

Losing at first instance is not the end of the road. Where there are proper grounds, we pursue appeals and reviews at every level of the South African court system.

What We Handle

  • Appeals to the High Court (full bench and single judge)
  • Appeals to the Supreme Court of Appeal
  • Petitions to the Constitutional Court
  • Reviews of Magistrate's Court decisions
  • Judicial review of administrative decisions
  • Applications for leave to appeal
  • Rescission applications

Cost-Conscious Approach

Appeals are expensive. Before we commit to one, we give you an honest assessment of the merits, the likely outcome, and the realistic costs, so you can make an informed decision about whether to fight, settle, or accept the judgment and move on.

How a Civil Matter Unfolds

Most civil matters follow a predictable path. Here's what to expect, and where we step in.

  1. Initial Consultation & Merits Assessment We review your documents, hear your version, and give you an honest view of whether you have a viable claim or defence, and whether litigation is worth the cost.
  2. Letter of Demand In most matters, a well-drafted letter of demand resolves the dispute without going to court. It signals that you're represented, serious, and prepared to litigate.
  3. Summons or Application Issued If the demand isn't met, we issue summons in the appropriate court or bring an application in motion court. The choice between action and application depends on the nature of the dispute.
  4. Pleadings & Discovery Both parties file pleadings setting out their claims and defences. Discovery is exchanged: each side discloses documents relevant to the matter. This is often where the real strength of each side becomes clear.
  5. Settlement Negotiations Most civil matters settle before trial, often during this stage. We negotiate firmly, with the full weight of a well-prepared case behind us, without overstating the position.
  6. Trial or Hearing If the matter doesn't settle, we present your case in court: examining and cross-examining witnesses, presenting legal argument, and persuading the court of your position.
  7. Judgment & Enforcement After judgment, we take the steps necessary to enforce the order: warrants of execution, garnishee orders, or whatever it takes to translate a paper victory into a real one.

Alternatives to Litigation

Court is not always the fastest, cheapest, or wisest route. Where a matter can be resolved another way, we say so, and pursue that route.

Negotiation

Direct, attorney-to-attorney negotiation. Often the fastest route to a commercial outcome, and usually the cheapest. We negotiate firmly, but realistically.

Mediation

A neutral third party helps both sides reach settlement. Confidential, non-binding, and often preserves commercial relationships that litigation would destroy.

Arbitration

A private tribunal gives a binding decision. Faster, more flexible, and more private than court, and commonly used in commercial and construction disputes.

Litigation FAQs

How much does civil litigation cost?

Costs vary widely depending on the complexity of the matter, the court involved, and whether the matter settles or goes to trial. A simple Magistrate's Court matter costs far less than a defended High Court trial, which can run to several hundred thousand rand. We provide a written cost estimate before you instruct us, and we keep you informed as the matter progresses. Where the claim is strong, we can also discuss contingency or capped-fee arrangements.

Can I recover my legal costs from the other side?

Often yes, but rarely the full amount. South African courts generally award costs on a party-and-party scale, which is significantly lower than the actual attorney-and-client fees you'll pay. Where the other side's conduct is found to be unreasonable, vexatious, or fraudulent, courts may award costs on a punitive scale (attorney-and-client or attorney-and-own-client). We always ask for the best costs order the facts support.

How long does a civil matter take?

Uncontested matters can be resolved in a few weeks. Defended matters in the Magistrate's Court typically take 9 to 18 months to reach trial; High Court matters often take 18 to 36 months. Urgent applications can be heard within days. Delays are common, because the court rolls in most centres are congested, but a well-run matter, with discovery and pleadings managed tightly, can move faster than the average.

What if I win but the other side can't pay?

A judgment is only as useful as the other side's ability to satisfy it. Before litigating, we assess whether the defendant has realisable assets. If they do, we enforce, through warrants of execution, garnishee orders, or other means. If they don't, we tell you early, so you can weigh whether pursuing a judgment is worth the cost. In some cases, a judgment still has value: for tax purposes, credit purposes, or as leverage in a negotiated settlement.

Can I bring a claim in the Small Claims Court?

The Small Claims Court handles claims up to R20,000. It is designed for individuals, not juristic persons, and you cannot be represented by an attorney in that court. If your claim falls within its jurisdiction and you're comfortable presenting your own case, it can be a fast and cheap route. If not, the Magistrate's Court is the next step up.

What is the difference between action and application proceedings?

Action proceedings involve a trial: pleadings, discovery, witness testimony, and cross-examination. They're used where the facts are disputed and need to be tested through evidence. Application proceedings are brought on affidavits alone, with no witnesses and no oral evidence. They're faster and cheaper, but only suitable where the facts are largely common cause or the dispute is primarily legal. Choosing the right procedure is one of the first strategic decisions we make.

Can I be sued in a province I don't live in?

Yes, but only in specific circumstances. Generally, a defendant can be sued where they reside or carry on business, where the contract was entered into or breached, or where the cause of action arose. If you're sued in the wrong court, we can raise a special plea of jurisdiction, but it must be raised early, or the right is lost. We always assess jurisdiction before filing.

What is prescription, and how long do I have to sue?

Prescription extinguishes the right to sue after a certain period. In most civil claims, the period is three years from the date the debt became due. Some claims have shorter periods (for example, certain claims against the state) and some longer. Once a claim has prescribed, it cannot be revived. If you think you have a claim, don't wait. Speak to us early so the necessary steps can be taken to interrupt prescription where appropriate.

Do you offer any alternative fee arrangements?

In suitable matters, yes. Depending on the strength of the claim and the profile of the parties, we may agree on a capped fee, a fixed fee for defined stages, or a contingency arrangement (a success fee calculated on the amount recovered). Contingency fees in South Africa are regulated by the Contingency Fees Act, so any such arrangement must comply strictly with the statutory requirements. We'll discuss what's possible at the outset.
