Will Updates and Amendments

Your will should evolve as your life changes. We help you keep your estate plan current and effective, ensuring it always reflects your latest wishes and circumstances.

When to Update Your Will

  • Marriage or divorce
  • Birth or adoption of children or grandchildren
  • Death of a beneficiary or executor
  • Significant changes in financial circumstances
  • Acquisition or disposal of major assets
  • Changes in tax laws affecting your estate
  • Relocation to a different province or country

Codicils vs New Wills

A codicil is a formal amendment to an existing will, suitable for minor changes. For significant revisions, we typically recommend drafting an entirely new will to avoid confusion and potential challenges. We'll advise you on the most appropriate approach for your situation.

Estate Planning Strategies

Beyond your will, a comprehensive estate plan uses multiple tools and strategies to protect your wealth, minimise taxes, and ensure smooth transfer of assets.

Inter Vivos Trusts

A trust established during your lifetime can provide significant benefits:

  • Asset protection from creditors and litigation
  • Estate duty reduction through strategic asset transfer
  • Probate avoidance for faster asset distribution
  • Privacy, trust assets are not part of the public estate record
  • Continuity of asset management if you become incapacitated
  • Protection for vulnerable beneficiaries

Types of trusts we establish:

  • Family trusts for wealth preservation
  • Business trusts for commercial asset protection
  • Special needs trusts for dependents with disabilities
  • Charitable trusts for philanthropic goals
  • Testamentary trusts (created through your will)

Life Insurance Planning

  • Adequate coverage assessment
  • Beneficiary designation review
  • Insurance trusts to manage policy proceeds
  • Estate duty funding through insurance

Retirement Fund Nominations

  • Pension and provident fund beneficiary nominations

  • Minor children's share management

Business Succession Planning

Donation and Gift Planning

Living Wills and Healthcare Directives

A living will ensures your healthcare wishes are known and respected if you become unable to communicate them yourself.

Living Will

  • Life support preferences and end-of-life decisions
  • Pain management and palliative care instructions
  • Organ donation preferences
  • Specific medical intervention decisions

Healthcare Power of Attorney

Appoint a trusted person to make medical decisions on your behalf if you become incapacitated. We ensure your healthcare proxy understands your values and wishes, and that the document is legally enforceable.

Powers of Attorney

A power of attorney allows someone you trust to act on your behalf in legal and financial matters. We draft powers of attorney that are clear, specific, and legally sound.

General Power of Attorney

  • Managing bank accounts and financial affairs
  • Handling property transactions
  • Signing legal documents
  • Managing business operations

Special Power of Attorney

  • Selling a specific property
  • Collecting a specific pension or benefit
  • Registering or transferring a vehicle
  • Representing you in a specific court matter

Enduring Power of Attorney

An enduring power of attorney continues to be effective even if you become mentally incapacitated. This is a critical document for ensuring your affairs are managed by someone you trust if you lose the ability to make decisions for yourself.

Why Estate Planning Matters

Without a Will

  • Estate administration delays of 18–24 months or more
  • Increased legal costs and executor fees
  • Family disputes over asset distribution
  • Courts appoint guardians, possibly not who you'd choose
  • Business disruption and potential failure
  • Tax inefficiency and unnecessary estate duty

With Proper Planning

  • You control who receives your assets and when
  • Your children are protected by guardians you've chosen
  • Tax-efficient structures minimise estate duty
  • Faster estate administration and distribution
  • Privacy through trust structures where appropriate
  • Peace of mind for you and your family

Estate Planning FAQs

Do I really need a will if I don't have much?

Yes. A will isn't just about distributing wealth; it's about making decisions. Who raises your children if something happens to you? Who handles your affairs? Without a will, these decisions are made by the courts according to a rigid formula that may not reflect your wishes. Even a modest estate benefits from a clear, legally valid will.

Can't I just use an online will template?

Online templates are generic and often don't account for South African legal requirements, your specific marital property regime, or complex family situations. A poorly drafted will can be challenged, declared invalid, or create unintended consequences that cost far more than professional drafting. We've seen many families face expensive litigation because of template wills that didn't properly address their circumstances.

What happens if I die without a will?

If you die without a valid will (intestate), the Intestate Succession Act determines how your assets are distributed. Your spouse and children inherit according to a fixed formula, which may not be what you would have chosen. The process takes longer, costs more, and the Master of the High Court appoints an administrator who may be a stranger to your family.

How often should I update my will?

We recommend reviewing your will every 3–5 years, or whenever a significant life event occurs: marriage, divorce, birth of a child, death of a beneficiary or executor, major asset acquisition or disposal, or relocation. Tax law changes may also necessitate updates to keep your estate plan efficient.

Should I appoint a family member or professional executor?

Both options have merits. A family member may understand your wishes and family dynamics, but estate administration is complex, time-consuming, and has strict legal requirements. A professional executor brings expertise and objectivity, particularly valuable for larger or more complex estates. We often recommend appointing a professional executor with a family member as co-executor for the best of both worlds.

What is estate duty and who pays it?

Estate duty is a tax of 20% on the dutiable value of an estate above R3.5 million (25% above R30 million). It is paid by the estate before assets are distributed to beneficiaries. With proper planning, including trusts, spousal bequests, and insurance, estate duty can be significantly reduced or even eliminated. The first R3.5 million is exempt, and bequests to a surviving spouse are deductible.

Can someone challenge my will?

Yes, a will can be challenged on several grounds: lack of testamentary capacity, undue influence, fraud, forgery, or improper execution. However, a professionally drafted will that follows proper procedures is much harder to challenge. We take every precaution to make sure your will can withstand a challenge, including thorough documentation of your capacity and intentions.

Estate Planning Checklist

  • I have a valid, up-to-date will
  • I have appointed a capable executor (and a backup)
  • I have appointed guardians for my minor children
  • All my assets are accounted for in my estate plan
  • I have considered whether a trust is appropriate for my situation
  • My life insurance coverage is adequate and beneficiaries are correct
  • My retirement fund nominations are up to date
  • I have a power of attorney in place
  • I have a living will with my healthcare wishes
  • My digital assets and online accounts are documented
  • My funeral and burial wishes are recorded
  • My original will is stored safely and my executor knows where it is
  • My executor has been informed of their appointment
  • My estate plan has been reviewed in the last 3 years
