What We Handle

Contracts & Policies

Written contracts and clear policies set expectations and make later decisions defensible.

What we handle

  • Employment contracts for permanent, fixed-term and part-time staff
  • Disciplinary, grievance, leave, remote work and conduct policies
  • Executive and senior management agreements
  • Probation, remuneration, incentive and bonus terms
  • Compliance with the Basic Conditions of Employment Act
Discipline & Misconduct

A fair dismissal needs a fair reason and a fair process. The form can be simple, but the steps must be there.

What we handle

  • Advising on whether the conduct justifies discipline or dismissal
  • Drafting charges, notices and hearing records
  • Chairing or advising the chairperson of disciplinary hearings
  • Final warnings, suspensions and settlement agreements
  • Workplace investigations into misconduct, harassment and fraud

We check the process before you act, because a procedural mistake can turn a justified dismissal into an unfair one.

Poor Performance & Incapacity

Dismissing for poor performance or ill health follows a different process from misconduct, with a duty to guide, support and consider alternatives.

What we handle

  • Performance management and improvement plans
  • Incapacity processes for ill health and injury
  • Medical evidence and accommodation
  • Fair termination when the process has been followed
Restructuring & Retrenchment

Operational changes need a consultation process before any dismissal.

What we handle

  • Section 189 and 189A consultation processes
  • Selection criteria, severance and notice
  • Alternatives to retrenchment, such as short-time and voluntary packages
  • Defending disputes about the process
Statutory Compliance

Employers must meet several overlapping statutory duties.

What we advise on

  • Employment equity duties and reporting, where they apply
  • Occupational health and safety and workplace injury compensation
  • Unemployment Insurance and skills levy obligations
  • Data protection and employee information under POPIA
  • Audit and risk reviews of your HR practices
Restraints & Confidentiality

A restraint of trade protects your business, but it is enforced only if it is reasonable and protects a legitimate interest.

What we handle

  • Drafting reasonable restraints and confidentiality undertakings
  • Enforcing restraints and obtaining urgent interdicts
  • Defending employees against restraints
  • Protecting trade secrets and client relationships
Representation at the CCMA & Labour Court

If a claim is referred, we represent the employer from conciliation to the Labour Court.

What we handle

  • CCMA conciliation and arbitration
  • Labour Court applications and reviews
  • Bargaining council disputes
  • Settlement negotiations

How We Work

  1. Review what you have We look at your contracts, policies and past disputes and tell you where the risk is.
  2. Fix the documents We update or draft the contracts and policies you need, in plain language your managers can use.
  3. Advise before you act We advise on each step in a dispute before it is taken, which is cheaper than correcting it afterwards.
  4. Run the process We prepare notices, run hearings and keep a clear record of the steps taken.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need written employment contracts?

The law requires employers to give employees written particulars of employment, and a proper contract is the best way to do that and to protect the business.

Can I dismiss someone immediately for serious misconduct?

Even in serious cases, you must follow a fair process, which usually includes notice of the allegations and a chance to respond. Instant dismissals are the most common cause of lost cases.

What is a fair retrenchment process?

A consultation process must start before dismissal, with notice, disclosure of relevant information, and genuine engagement on alternatives and selection. Severance pay is at least one week for each completed year of service.

Are restraints of trade enforceable?

They can be, if they protect a legitimate interest such as client connections or confidential information, and are reasonable in scope, area and duration. A court will not enforce one that is wider than it needs to be.

How can I reduce the risk of CCMA claims?

Have clear contracts and policies, apply them consistently, keep records and take advice before dismissing. Small steps taken early prevent most of the cost.
