Family Law

Divorce, children, maintenance and protection orders, handled with discretion and a clear plan from the first consultation.

Home / Practice Areas / Family Law

Family disputes are emotional, and they are often urgent. Decisions made in the first weeks, about where the children live, who stays in the home and what is agreed in writing, tend to shape the outcome for years. We give you a clear picture of your options and the likely costs before you commit, and we aim to settle a matter where that protects you and your children. Where a matter has to be fought, we appear in the Magistrates' Courts, the Regional Courts and the High Court.

What We Handle

Divorce & Separation

A court grants a divorce once it is satisfied that the marriage has broken down irretrievably. Most divorces can be settled by agreement, which is faster and cheaper than a trial.

What we handle

  • Uncontested divorces and settlement agreements
  • Contested divorces, including division of the joint estate and accrual claims
  • Spousal maintenance and claims for forfeiture of patrimonial benefits
  • Rule 43 applications for interim maintenance, contact and a contribution to legal costs
  • Tracing, valuing and protecting assets, including pensions and business interests
  • Setting aside or varying existing orders where circumstances have changed

Your marital regime

  • Whether you married in or out of community of property, and with or without accrual, decides how assets are divided
  • If you signed an antenuptial contract, we start by reading it
  • Our notaries can also advise on antenuptial contracts through our Notarial Services

Fault is not required to obtain a divorce, but conduct can matter for forfeiture and for maintenance. We advise early on what a court is likely to do.

Children: Care, Contact & Guardianship

The Children's Act 38 of 2005 puts the best interests of the child first. Parents hold parental responsibilities and rights, and the law does not favour mothers over fathers or the reverse.

What we handle

  • Parenting plans and agreements on where children live and when they see each parent
  • Applications for care, contact and guardianship
  • Relocation disputes, including moves between provinces or abroad
  • Consent to travel and passport disputes
  • Parental rights of unmarried fathers
  • Children's Court matters and reports from the Office of the Family Advocate

Courts decide on the facts: who has cared for the child, the child's needs and each parent's ability to meet them. A well-drafted parenting plan often avoids a court fight altogether.

Maintenance

Both parents have a duty to support their children, according to their means. Maintenance is dealt with in the Maintenance Court, in a divorce, or by agreement made an order of court.

What we handle

  • Applications to fix, increase or reduce maintenance
  • Enforcing orders, including attachment of salary and prosecution for default
  • Recovering arrears
  • Maintenance for children who cannot yet support themselves, for example while studying
  • Spousal maintenance in or after a divorce
  • Responding to maintenance claims against you

A maintenance order can be changed if circumstances change materially, for example loss of income or a change in the child's needs.

Protection Orders & Domestic Violence

The Domestic Violence Act 116 of 1998 protects people in domestic relationships from physical, sexual, emotional, verbal, psychological and economic abuse, as well as intimidation, harassment and stalking.

What we handle

  • Applications for interim and final protection orders at the Magistrate's Court
  • Opposing an application or applying to set an order aside
  • Enforcing orders where they are breached
  • Protection from Harassment Act applications where the parties are not in a domestic relationship
  • Urgent applications to keep you and your children safe

There is no court fee to apply for a protection order. If you are in danger now, call the SAPS on 10111. The GBV Command Centre is open around the clock on 0800 428 428.

Relationship & Property Agreements

People live together, marry under customary law or enter civil unions. Each arrangement has legal consequences for property, maintenance and inheritance.

What we handle

  • Life partnership and cohabitation agreements
  • Registering and proving customary marriages
  • Civil union agreements
  • Applications to change the matrimonial property system
  • Wills and estate planning that fit your family structure, through our Wills & Estates team

How We Work

  1. Confidential consultation We listen to your situation, explain your rights and give you an honest view of the options and the likely cost.
  2. Urgent protection If safety, children or money are at immediate risk, we act first, with a protection order, an interim order or an urgent application.
  3. Settlement first We negotiate directly or through mediation. A signed settlement agreement is usually quicker, cheaper and less damaging than a trial.
  4. Court if needed If the other side will not settle, we issue and run the matter in the right court and keep you informed at each stage.
  5. Order and enforcement We make sure the final order or agreement is made an order of court, and enforce it if it is not followed.

What to Bring to Your First Consultation

Having these ready lets us give you a faster, more useful assessment. We will confirm exactly what is needed once we understand your situation.

  • Identity documents for you and your spouse or partner
  • Marriage certificate, and any antenuptial contract
  • Birth certificates of your children
  • Three months of payslips and bank statements
  • A list of assets and debts, with approximate values
  • Details of current care and contact arrangements
  • Any court orders, protection orders or letters already received
  • A short timeline of key events
  • Your monthly household and child expenses

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a divorce take?

An uncontested divorce, where both spouses sign a settlement agreement, can be finalised in a matter of weeks to a few months once papers are issued. A contested divorce takes much longer, often a year or more, depending on the issues and the court roll.

Do I have to prove my spouse was at fault?

No. The ground for divorce is that the marriage has broken down irretrievably. Conduct can still matter when a court decides on forfeiture of benefits or maintenance.

Who will the children live with?

The court decides in the best interests of each child. There is no automatic preference for either parent. Parents are encouraged to agree a parenting plan, and the Family Advocate may be asked to report in a contested matter.

Can a maintenance order be changed?

Yes. Either parent can apply to vary an order if their circumstances or the child's needs have changed materially.

What does it cost to get a protection order?

There is no court fee to apply. You do not need an attorney for the first application, but we can prepare it, represent you at the return date and deal with any breach.
