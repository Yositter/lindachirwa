What We Handle

Sale of a Business

In a sale of business, the buyer acquires the assets and, usually, the liabilities it agrees to take, as a going concern.

What we handle

  • Letters of intent, term sheets and confidentiality agreements
  • Sale of business agreements, including warranties and indemnities
  • Transferring contracts, licences, premises and stock
  • Employee transfers and VAT and tax treatment of a going concern
  • Restraints of trade for sellers and key people
Share Sales & Investments

In a share sale, the buyer takes over the company itself, with its history and liabilities.

What we handle

  • Share purchase agreements
  • Bringing in investors and drafting subscription agreements
  • Management buy-outs and buy-ins
  • Conditions precedent and timelines to closing
  • Securities transfer and share register updates
Due Diligence

Due diligence is the legal and commercial check a buyer runs before committing, and the one a seller should run first.

What we review

  • Corporate records, shareholdings and authority to sell
  • Material contracts and change-of-control clauses
  • Property, leases and licences
  • Employment and labour liabilities
  • Litigation, regulatory compliance and intellectual property
  • Tax and debt that could transfer with the business

Findings feed the price, the warranties and the conditions of the deal, so we report plainly on what matters.

Approvals & Consents

Some transactions cannot close until third parties approve them.

What we advise on

  • Shareholder resolutions, including special resolutions required when a company disposes of all or most of its assets
  • Competition law notification and approval, where the size of the parties makes the transaction notifiable
  • Landlord, funder and counterparty consents
  • Sector regulator approvals where relevant
Employees, Tax & Property

These issues are often overlooked until late in the deal.

What we advise on

  • The transfer of employees on the same terms when a business is sold as a going concern
  • VAT, capital gains tax, transfer duty and securities transfer tax
  • Transferring immovable property, with our conveyancing team
  • Structuring the price, including deferred and earn-out payments
Closing & After

Closing is a process, not a signature.

What we handle

  • Closing checklists and signing arrangements
  • Payment mechanics, escrow and retention of part of the price
  • Handover documents and register updates
  • Post-closing disputes over warranties and price adjustments

How We Work

  1. Terms and confidentiality We settle a confidentiality agreement and record the main commercial terms.
  2. Due diligence We review the target business, or prepare yours for review, and report on the issues.
  3. Negotiate the agreement We draft and negotiate the agreement, with warranties, conditions and protections that match the risks found.
  4. Satisfy the conditions We manage approvals, consents and the other steps that must happen before closing.
  5. Close and hand over We close the deal, move the money and assets, and attend to post-closing matters.

What to Bring to Your First Consultation

Having these ready lets us give you a faster, more useful assessment. We will confirm exactly what is needed once we understand your situation.

  • The company's registration documents, share register and Memorandum of Incorporation

  • Tax clearance and compliance records

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I sell the business or the shares?

It depends on tax, risk and the buyer's goals. Buyers often prefer an asset sale because they can leave liabilities behind. Sellers often prefer a share sale for tax reasons. We model both with your accountant.

What is due diligence?

It is the structured review a buyer carries out to check what it is buying. Results can change the price, the warranties or whether the deal proceeds.

What happens to the employees?

When a business is transferred as a going concern, section 197 of the Labour Relations Act transfers the employees to the buyer automatically, on the same terms, and the seller and buyer share certain liabilities.

Does the deal need Competition Commission approval?

Only if the parties meet the size thresholds for a notifiable merger. We check this at the start, because a notifiable deal cannot close without approval.

What are warranties and indemnities?

Warranties are statements the seller makes about the business. If they are untrue, the buyer can claim. Indemnities protect the buyer against specific known risks.
