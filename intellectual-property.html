What We Handle

Trade Marks

A trade mark identifies the origin of your goods or services. Registration under the Trade Marks Act 194 of 1993 gives you the exclusive right to use the mark for the goods and services it covers.

What we handle

  • Searches to check whether a name or logo is available
  • Filing applications with CIPC in the right classes
  • Responding to examination reports and oppositions
  • Renewals, which fall due every ten years
  • Opposing or cancelling conflicting marks
  • Recording assignments and licences

Register before you spend on branding. A search first can save you from rebranding later.

Copyright

Copyright protects original literary, artistic and musical works, films, software and more. It arises automatically when the work is created, and no registration is needed.

What we handle

  • Advising on who owns a work, including works made by employees and contractors
  • Assignments and licences of copyright
  • Copyright notices and website content
  • Software and database ownership
  • Takedown notices and infringement claims

For most literary and artistic works, copyright lasts for the author's life and 50 years after death. Without a written assignment, a commissioned creator often keeps the copyright.

Patents & Designs

A patent protects a new invention that involves an inventive step and can be applied in industry. A registered design protects the look of an article, or its function in some cases.

What we handle

  • Advising on whether an invention is patentable and when to disclose it
  • Working with patent attorneys on drafting and filing
  • Design registrations under the Designs Act 195 of 1993
  • Licensing and commercialising inventions
  • Disputes over ownership and infringement

Patents last up to 20 years from filing, subject to annual renewal fees. Public disclosure before filing can destroy your right, so speak to us before you publish or pitch.

Enforcement & Disputes

When someone copies your brand or work, speed and the right remedy matter.

What we handle

  • Letters of demand and cease and desist notices
  • Urgent interdicts to stop infringement and passing off
  • Damages and the surrender of profits
  • Counterfeit goods complaints under the Counterfeit Goods Act 37 of 1997
  • Defending allegations of infringement against you
Licensing, Assignments & Ownership

Intellectual property creates value only when ownership and rights of use are clear.

What we handle

  • Licence and franchise agreements
  • Assignments of trade marks, copyright and designs
  • IP clauses in employment and contractor agreements
  • IP due diligence when buying or selling a business, with our M&A team
  • Confidentiality and trade secret protection
Domain Names & Online Brand

Your online presence can be hijacked as easily as it is built.

What we handle

  • Disputes over .co.za and other domain names
  • Social media account and handle disputes
  • Online impersonation and brand misuse
  • Terms for websites, marketplaces and influencers

How We Work

  1. Identify what you have We list your brands, works and inventions, and who owns each of them.
  2. Search and advise We search the registers, advise on the strongest form of protection and explain the cost.
  3. File and register We prepare and file applications, respond to examiners and track each one to registration.
  4. Put ownership in writing We update employment, contractor and licence agreements so ownership is clear.
  5. Monitor and enforce We diarise renewals and act quickly when we see an infringement.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to register copyright?

No. Copyright arises automatically when an original work is created and recorded. Keep dated drafts and files, because they are your evidence of authorship.

How long does trade mark registration take?

Often a year or more, depending on examination and whether anyone opposes. Your application date still protects your priority while it is pending.

Can I use the ® symbol?

Only once the mark is registered. You can use ™ for an unregistered mark that you are using or have applied to register.

Who owns work created by an employee?

For works made in the course of employment, the employer is generally the first owner of copyright, subject to any agreement to the contrary. Contractors are different, so we put it in writing.

What can I do if someone is copying my brand?

Collect evidence, and then contact us. Depending on your rights, we can send a demand, apply for an urgent interdict and claim damages.
